# Autoland, 2026-10-24

Goal: a mode the pilot can switch on (`L`, or the AUTOLAND button beside
NEAREST AIRPORT) that lands the aeroplane at the nearest airport inside the
flight model's physics, and goes around and tries again when an approach
cannot end in a landing. This note records how it works, what was measured,
and what the flight model still makes hard.

## How it flies

`src/sim/autoland.ts` writes the same `FlightControls` a pilot writes; the
simulator integrates them unchanged. Nothing sets a position, attitude or
speed, so stall, thrust, drag, inertia, actuator rates, gear and flap travel
and tyre friction bind it exactly as they bind a pilot.

1. **Plan.** For both runway ends and a range of final lengths, the shortest
   path of the aeroplane's own turn radius (`src/sim/dubins.ts`) joins its
   position and track to the start of the final. A candidate is refused if it
   cannot lose the height and speed it has before the gate, if terrain along
   it (or along the final, `longestClearFinal`) is too high, or if the final
   is longer than the clear ground in front of the runway. A start too high
   and close for any straight-in plan picks a longer one, a loop or orbits:
   that is the "loop around".
2. **Guide.** L1 lateral guidance on the path; a vertical profile that
   becomes the 3-degree glideslope to an aim point 300 m in for the trainer,
   about 210 m for the 747. A terrain look-ahead along the velocity (0 to 60 s,
   refreshed twice a second) raises the floor in the approach and makes a
   go-around climb steeper when there is rising ground ahead.
3. **Inner loops.** Bank, flight path and speed become surface and throttle
   commands by inverting the flight model's own force and moment equations,
   with the per-aeroplane numbers taken from `AircraftDefinition`. The load
   factor is capped where the angle of attack would reach its ceiling, below
   the stall. The autothrottle reads the flight path through the air, so a
   downdraft adds power instead of removing it.
4. **Gates.** Below `clamp(2.5 * Vapp, 90, 200)` m the approach must be on
   the glideslope and centreline, inside its speed band and sink rate, fully
   configured and wings level. Failing a gate, landing long or bouncing is a
   go-around: full power, climb, clean up, plan again. After six approaches it
   hands the controls back.
5. **Rollout.** The nose is lowered at a pitch rate that slows as it nears
   the runway, then the autobrake holds a deceleration aimed at stopping
   250 m before the far end (at least 0.36 g since 2026-10-25), while the
   pedals steer it back to the centreline and, by walking pace, square to
   the runway.

## Measured

`scripts/autoland-probe.mts` flies the same flights headless
(`npx tsx scripts/autoland-probe.mts <kind> <spawn|away|short-high|far>`,
`BURST=<m/s>` adds the test's microburst). World seed `0x51a7e`, real terrain
and wind, runway 1320 m. Sink is at main-gear touchdown; impact is the
hardest contact of any wheel, nosewheel included; stop is metres past the
threshold and right of the centreline.

| trainer | pointed away | 1 | 0.66 | 312 | 0.77 | 464 (0.1) |

`tests/sim.autoland.test.ts` pins these flights (13 of them, plus the profile
and a refusal to engage on the ground) against limits: no crash, stopped on
the runway, sink under 1.2 m/s, touchdown between 150 m and 40% of the
runway, impact under 1.6 m/s, angle of attack under the stall, bank inside the
planner's limit. `tests/sim.autoland-dubins.test.ts` holds the path planner to
its goal pose over 60 start and goal pairs at three turn radii.

Cost, in the worker: planning takes about 1 ms for the trainer and 2.4 ms for
the 747 (median), and runs at engagement, after a go-around and at most every
6 s before the final. A step of `update` takes 3 to 4 microseconds and
allocates nothing.

## What the flight model still makes hard

- **Brakes and tyres were viscous and not scaled by mass** (rolling force
  `|v| * (450 + 7000 * brake)` and side force `|v| * 8000` per leg), so a
  braked tyre was close to an isotropic damper and the 747 stopped 2 to 6 m
  right of centre, up to 6 degrees off the runway heading. Fixed on
  2026-10-25 with friction brakes, a friction circle and load-scaled
  cornering; the rollout now stops every type square to the runway. See
  GROUND_HANDLING_2026_10_25.md, which also has this table re-measured after
  that day's firmer, quicker autoland.
- **The 747 rocks on its gear and drifts about 49 m over a roll with neutral
  controls**, autoland or not. A sim-model issue.
- **The runway is short for a 747**: 1320 m, against a certified landing
  field length of roughly 2 km for the real aeroplane. With friction brakes
  (2026-10-25) it fits at the autobrake's 0.36 g, stopping about 1000 m
  past the threshold.
- **The bizjet's and 747's nosewheels touch at 1.1 to 1.5 m/s**: the ground
  spoilers pitch the nose down faster than the derotation law can hold it.
