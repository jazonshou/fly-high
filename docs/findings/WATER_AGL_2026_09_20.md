# AGL over water: what changed, and why lakes did not

**Status: shipped on `jazonshou/water-surface-agl`.**

Jason's report, verbatim: *"I noticed that when the plane is above the water,
the AGL is calculated relative to the seafloor rather than the water. Please
change it so it's relative to the water's surface."* And, on scope: *"Hitting
water shouldn't count as a crash for now - nothing should change about impact -
you should just fix the AGL calculation. Make it negative if the plane goes
under water."*

The instrument was reporting a surface the pilot cannot see and will never
touch. Over open ocean the bed sits 50–105 m below the waterline on the shelf
profile, and up to ~4 km on the abyssal one.

## What it is now

One exported authority, `pilotSurfaceClearance(terrainClearance, lowestPointY,
seaLevel)` in `src/sim/simulation.ts`, because **there are two readouts**: the
simulator's, from the aircraft's contact points, and the terrain viewer's,
computed from the camera in `src/game/freeFly.ts`. They had separate arithmetic
that agreed everywhere except over water — which is how the viewer HUD came to
show a height above the seabed in the screenshot that started the glitter work.

Clearance above **terrain** is clamped at zero, as it always was: suspension
compression puts tyre contact points slightly below the ideal ground plane, and
a runway should read exactly 0. Clearance above **water** is signed. The reading
is the smaller of the two, which is what makes it correct at a coastline
straddle — nose over land, tail over water — and continuous across the
shoreline, since as the terrain rises to meet the datum the two converge.

**Display only.** Contact, friction, impact and crash keep sampling the real
terrain. This was deliberately NOT done by wrapping the worker's terrain
sampler, which would have been a one-liner: that sampler also feeds
`couldReachTerrain`, the broad-phase gate in front of the entire contact and
crash path, and raising it to the waterline would open the broad phase over
every ocean column in the world.

## What happens when you fly into the sea (unchanged, for the record)

The sea surface does not exist to the physics at all. The aircraft passes
through it as through air and keeps descending until its gear or airframe
reaches the **seabed**, where the ordinary land contact model runs unchanged —
suspension, the 8.5 m/s crash threshold, wreck placement. There is no buoyancy,
water drag, splash, ditching term or surface crash trigger anywhere in
`src/sim`. It flies marginally *better* underwater: air density is
`standardAirDensity(position.y)` clamped at −500 m, so below sea level the air
is at most ~6% denser.

Two sea-aware lines already existed and both are correct as they stand.
`terrainGrid.ts:105` (friction 0.05 below sea level) is a wheels-on-the-seabed
tyre-slickness rule, consulted only inside `applyGroundForces` with gear down
and a wheel within suspension reach — it does nothing at the surface and nothing
in flight. And crash **recovery** already respects the sea, respawning above
`max(seaLevel, terrain)` (`src/game/spawn.ts:81`).

Visually, flying under the sea shades the water ceiling correctly from below
(`applyUnderwaterBeerLambert`, guarded by `cameraBelow`) and changes nothing
else: no underwater post-process, no water-column attenuation on terrain,
aircraft or sky, and fog is `FOGMODE_NONE` and asserted so.

## Lakes and rivers: deferred, and the reason is structural

**There is no pure function of world position for a lake surface, in either
world mode.** This is not a matter of effort; it is the shape of the data.

A lake's surface is `basin.terrainHeight + waterDepth`, where `waterDepth` is
derived from an enclosure depth measured over 32 radial rays (~900 terrain
samples per candidate). The basin is not a property of the query point: it is
the **terminus of a downhill trace** started from a global source-lattice cell,
running up to `maximumTraceSteps × traceStepMeters` = 180 × 90 m = **16.2 km**.
So "which lake is under (x, z)?" cannot be answered without tracing every source
cell within ~16 km of it — which is the region generation, a bounded job with a
300,000-sample ceiling of which base tracing alone consumes 96%.

Generation is whole-region and lives on the render thread: results are held per
resident region by `HydrologySystem`, whose only public read is the **current
region's** lake list, swapped asynchronously with a default region extent of
14.4 km. Eroded worlds take a different route entirely — lakes come from the
macro channel graph — and the macro that reaches the sim worker
(`TerrainMacroGrid`) carries heights only, no lake mask and no spill elevation.

**What plumbing it would take**, if someone decides the magnitude justifies it:
a new render→sim command (the protocol already has precedent in
`terrainPage`/`terrainMacro`), a resident-lake cache in the sim worker, and
acceptance that the answer is only as fresh as the resident page — an aircraft
that outruns the page has no lake data at all. The lake records are plain
structured-cloneable data and there is a ready point-in-lake primitive
(`distanceToRingMeters`), so it is genuinely possible, just not free.

**The magnitude argument for not doing it yet.** `maximumLakeDepthMeters` is 16
over a radius of at most 900 m, so a lake's terrain-vs-surface error is bounded
at ~16 m over a small patch. Rivers carry a 0.16 m surface offset, and the
hydrology source records a re-measured **zero rivers** in analytic worlds across
five seeds. The sea is the case worth 50–105 m, and it is the one that shipped.

## Residuals, named rather than left to be found

- **Wheels spin if you hover under 0.35 m above the sea.** The wheels-rolling
  test is `onGround || (agl >= 0 && agl < 0.35)`; the lower bound is new and
  stops the tyres spinning all the way to the seabed on a now-negative reading.
  What it cannot do is tell "0.2 m above water" from "0.2 m above a runway"
  without a second telemetry field, which is not worth a protocol change for one
  cosmetic frame of a case that needs a float plane to reach.
- **The reading is blind to waves.** The datum is flat — the ocean mesh is drawn
  at `seaLevel` and the curvature drop was deliberately withdrawn — while the
  rendered surface oscillates about it with a significant wave height of
  0.9–2.6 m. So the number is right on average and off by about a metre
  instantaneously. Matching a crest would mean reading back GPU displacement.
- **Lakes and rivers stay on the bed datum**, per the section above, so the
  pilot-facing AGL is correct over the sea and up to ~16 m optimistic over a
  lake. Known-wrong and written down rather than discovered later.

## Consumers that moved

- **The attract-mode autopilot resets the demo below 65 m AGL** — and with the
  seabed as the datum, over deep water it *never reset*, because the aircraft
  could be anywhere from 75 m to ~3,900 m below the surface and still read above
  65. The menu flight could wander under the sea indefinitely. This change fixes
  that in passing.
- **Landing lights** (`<= 900 m`) move from wrongly-excluded to correctly-
  included between ~900 and ~1,005 m over the sea. No rendered pixel changes
  today because `landingSwitchOn` is hardcoded `false`.
- **The HUD was already negative-ready**: `Math.max(-999, Math.round(altitude))
  .toLocaleString()` renders the minus sign in both feet and metres and floors
  at −999, and no layout or styling is keyed on altitude.
- **The perf-capture harness is untouched**, for two independent reasons: it
  places shot cameras from its own terrain sampler and never from the sim's
  telemetry, and every water shot is placed by MSL rather than AGL anyway.
